### [1] [Resistive wall mode induced disruptions in an advanced tokamak](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02349)
*Sui Wan,Ping Zhu*

Main category: physics.plasm-ph

TL;DR: 通过NIMROD代码对CFETR基准平衡进行三维MHD模拟，揭示了电阻壁模驱动托卡马克破裂的完整过程：从线性阶段的n=1 RWM主导，到非线性阶段的磁面随机化、热淬灭和电流淬灭。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 电阻壁模是托卡马克在无壁βN限以上发生破裂的主要原因之一。理解RWM驱动的破裂过程对于预测和缓解托卡马克装置中的破裂至关重要，特别是在先进托卡磁配置中。

Method: 使用NIMROD代码对CFETR基准稳态平衡进行非线性三维电阻MHD模拟，并通过线性计算验证n=1 RWM不稳定性的主导地位。

Result: 线性计算证实了n=1 RWM不稳定性的主导存在；在非线性阶段，RWM驱动了快速的磁面层随机化和热淬灭；电流淬灭前出现的瞬态电流尖峰源于极向磁通守恒和内电感的快速减小；在电流淬灭后期，封闭磁面从核心区到边缘部分重构并趋向无力状态。

Conclusion: 本研究通过非线性三维电阻MHD模拟揭示了电阻壁模驱动的托卡马克破裂过程的完整机制。研究发现，在高S数极限下，n=1 RWM的增长率对壁响应敏感且与等离子体电阻率弱相关。在非线性阶段，RWM导致磁面层快速随机化、热淬灭和随后由Spitzer电阻率增加加剧的电流淬灭。该研究为先进托卡马克配置中的RWM驱动破裂过程提供了全面的MHD模型理解。

Abstract: Resistive wall mode is one of the leading causes for tokamak disruptions above the no-wall $β_N$ limit. This paper presents nonlinear three-dimensional resistive MHD simulations of an RWM-induced disruption in a CFETR baseline steady-state equilibrium using the NIMROD code. Linear calculations confirm the dominant presence of the $n=1$ RWM instability, whose growth rate is strongly sensitive to the wall response and becomes weakly dependent on plasma resistivity in the high-$S$ limit, along with a global external-kink-like structure. In the nonlinear phase, the RWM drives rapid flux surface stochastization and a thermal quench, followed by a current quench that is intensified by the post quench increase of Spitzer resistivity. The transient current spike before the current quench is shown to be the outcome of the conservation of poloidal flux and a rapid reduction of internal inductance. During the late current quench stage, closed flux surfaces partially reform from the core region to the edge, relaxing toward the force-free state. Toroidal mode coupling, parallel heat transport, plasma resistivity, and wall conductivity strongly modulate the disruption onset and the quench dynamics. Within the MHD model, these results provide a complete view on the RWM-driven disruption process in advanced tokamak configurations.
