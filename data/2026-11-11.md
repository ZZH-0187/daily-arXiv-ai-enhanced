### [1] [Compact plasma-based extreme compression of relativistic electron beams](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13436)

Abstract: Accelerated particle beams are essential tools of modern science and technology with applications ranging from medicine [1], to light sources [2], to high-energy physics [3]. Over time, as accelerator-based experiments become more sophisticated and probe natural phenomena with greater temporal resolution, there is an increasing demand on beam parameters, in particular on bunch duration. We demonstrate the first application of this plasma-compressed bunch using it to drive single-microbunch free electron lasing at optical wavelengths in a single, meter-scale undulator module, producing radiation pulses roughly four orders of magnitude more energetic than the first beam-driven, plasma-based free electron laser demonstration at the same accelerator facility [5, 6].

Abstract: In plasmas, frequent soft Coulomb collisions gradually transfer energy and momentum, whereas a single close collision can change both abruptly and redirect a particle. This coexistence can shape runaway-electron avalanches in tokamaks and alpha-particle heating in inertial fusion, yet no first-principles analytic theory has determined how the cumulative statistics evolve under plasma conditions. Fokker--Planck and moment-matched models reproduce average transport but do not determine how scattering evolves from isolated events to cumulative relaxation across plasma conditions. Here we resolve this long-standing problem directly from Boltzmann theory. By asymptotically rescaling the exact angular expansion, we obtain the first quantitative map of the single-collision, core-tail coexistence, and low-order relaxation regimes within a unified Boltzmann framework. The theory further shows that small-angle models differ from the Boltzmann distribution even in the forward core, so appending a large-angle tail to a conventional core cannot recover the collision statistics. In fast-electron transport, this full-range redistribution leaves mean energy loss nearly unchanged yet counterintuitively enhances beam concentration and modifies the energy flux delivered to a finite region.
