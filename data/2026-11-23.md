Abstract: Ignition of the shocked deuterium tritium shell is identified during the thermonuclear combustion of very high-gain targets in inertial confinement fusion (ICF). This mechanism is due to the local energy deposition of the fusion alphas created in this shocked shell, independently of hot-spot ignition, and generates sufficient energy to fully recompress the hot spot. A new analytical criterion for the ignition of the hot layer is presented. A simple three zone model, based on the thick layer hot-spot model, reproduces this mechanism, which breaks down into successive stages: ignition of the hot spot, compression of the shocked shell, ignition of the shocked shell, and subsequent recompression of the hot spot. 1D numerical simulations highlight the onset of this hot layer ignition thereby satisfying the analytical ignition criterion. It is shown that this mechanism amplifies the energy released by the combustion of the target by approximately 50 %.
